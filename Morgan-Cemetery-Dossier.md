# Morgan Cemetery / CemDevCo / Louis Cicalese — Research Dossier

## Purpose and sourcing standard

This dossier gathers public records on Morgan Cemetery (Cinnaminson Township, Burlington County, NJ), its operator Cemetery Development Company, Inc. ("CemDevCo"), and its president Louis Cicalese. It is meant for an attorney or the township reviewing burials placed at the cemetery's boundary with an adjoining private lot.

- **Sourcing:** only public sources. Every factual claim links to the page it came from.
- **Findings vs. allegations:** each item is labeled. **Finding** = a regulator, court or the party itself admitted or decided it. **Allegation** = a complaint, a lawsuit claim or a press report not yet decided.
- **Limits:** this is research, not legal advice. Online sources are incomplete; court dockets and Cemetery Board files must be pulled directly (see the records-request section).
- **Subject's side:** where the operator or its attorneys responded publicly, that response is included.

## Executive summary

Cinnaminson already found Morgan in violation: an April 7, 2025 enforcement notice says its perimeter burials lack Planning Board approval and a zoning permit and ignore R-2 setbacks (15 ft side, 25 ft rear). About 20 burials have followed. The Township's stated reason for not enforcing is the cemetery's grandfather claim, which is a defense to argue in court, not a bar. Separately, Morgan may be burying on land it was never approved to use. State law bars enlarging a cemetery without a township consent resolution and Cemetery Board approval ([N.J.S.A. 45:27-25](https://lis.njleg.state.nj.us/nxt/gateway.dll/statutes/1/41981/44260?f=templates$fn=document-frame.htm$3.0)).

- **Operator:** Morgan Cemetery (Cinnaminson, founded 1888) is run by Louis Cicalese, president, through his company CemDevCo, which runs about 18 NJ cemeteries and crematories.
- **Track record:** In 1996 Cicalese and a partner paid a **$60,000 state fine** over burials under sidewalks, up to nine people per grave, and resold graves. Since then his cemeteries have drawn complaints about crowding new graves among old ones (Warren County, 2018–2020), work without local permits, and burials on disputed land (Tinton Falls, 2023–2026). These are allegations and open investigations, not findings.
- **Towns have acted:** Tinton Falls joined a lawsuit against him in 2023 (MON-L-1120-23). Washington Borough took complaints to the Board, and residents there blocked a 125-acre expansion in 2012.
- **"Grandfathering":** The Township's notice treats perimeter burials as a new or changed use of land that needs a permit. Nonconforming status generally protects only the use as it existed. Whether Morgan's whole tract counts as "in use" since 1888 is the key legal question.
- **Money:** Morgan owes Cicalese's own trust millions on a land certificate that grave sales repay. It paid the trust about $2.2M in 2024–2025, leaving about $60K in operating cash. Its board is made up of his sister, employees of his company, and their accountant.
- **Next steps:** Get a boundary survey, then file OPRA requests for any consent resolution, the dedication deed, the cemetery map, and the Board's written decision. The checklist is near the end of this document.

## Morgan Cemetery: history, control and money

Morgan Cemetery is a 138-year-old nonprofit cemetery at 1100 Cinnaminson Avenue. Since at least 2016 it has been controlled by Cicalese, who is its president. The original dedicated boundary is the fact the boundary question turns on, and it is not online.

**History.** William F. Morgan founded it in 1888 ([Morgan Cemetery](https://www.morgancemeteryassoc.com/); [MapQuest listing](https://www.mapquest.com/us/new-jersey/morgan-cemetery-352689965)). Search excerpts of digitized local papers mention burials there by 1892, and "Morgan Cemetery, Inc." appears in a 1929 paper ([Riverton Historical Society archive, 1892](https://rivertonhistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/THE-WEEKLY-NEWS-1892-01.pdf); [1929](https://rivertonhistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/THE-NEW-ERA-1929-12.pdf)). Its own homepage wrongly places it in Cherry Hill, which suggests the CemDevCo site template was reused without much care.

**Who controls it.** The operating nonprofit is Morgan-Cemetery Inc., EIN 22-2854827, a 501(c)(13) cemetery company listed in Camden. Its IRS exemption was issued in April 2015 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/222854827)). Camden is CemDevCo's office address. 2025 officers: Louis Cicalese (president), Michael Baratta (vice president), and trustees Michael Carvin, David Jacobs and Michelle Cicalese.

**Money (Form 990, from ProPublica).** Cicalese takes no pay from Morgan directly. He reports "related" pay from affiliated organizations of $15,000 to $236,000 a year.

## Township enforcement notice (April 7, 2025)

Cinnaminson's zoning office has already found that Morgan broke the township code by burying in perimeter areas without approval. The cemetery says it is grandfathered. That is a defense it can raise in court, not a bar to the Township filing charges. About 20 burials have followed the notice, according to the adjoining owner.

**Site facts from the adjoining owner (to be backed by photos and a survey).**

- The burials are in a strip of grass and dirt about **15 feet wide** between the owner's fence and the cemetery road.
- When the owner moved in, in **2017**, that strip had **no graves**. Burials there began about a year and a half ago.
- About **20 graves** have been added since the April 7, 2025 notice.

These facts matter for two reasons. First, if the whole strip is only about 15 ft deep, every grave in it falls inside the 15-ft side-yard and 25-ft rear-yard minimums the notice cites. Second, a strip with no burials from at least 2017 until recently makes a strong case that this is new use of ground, not continuation of an existing use. Dated photos from 2017 onward (your own, real-estate listing photos, Google Street View or aerial history) would prove it.

**What the notice found.** It covers 1100 Cinnaminson Ave., Block 1608, Lot 1, Zone R-2. The owner of record is "Morgan Cemetery" at 1640 Haddon Ave., Camden, which is CemDevCo's office. It states that the cemetery's operators "have recently elected, without the required approval from the Planning Board, to utilize perimeter areas of the tract as areas for gravesites and burial places without the required approvals and without consideration for various setbacks distances from neighboring residents." The violation followed "complaints from neighboring residents."

| Code section | What it requires | How the notice applies it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| § 525-6 | After the chapter took effect, no one may "occupy or use any land" except in conformity with the chapter | Perimeter burials are a use of land that must conform |
| § 525-20(B) | Cemeteries are a conditional use, subject to Planning Board approval | No Planning Board approval was obtained |
| § 525-137 | A zoning permit is required to "begin or change the use of any land" | Cited as "Occupancy and/or Utilization of Land without Required Permits" |
| § 525-21(D) | R-2 side yards: 15 ft minimum each, 40 ft combined | Cited as "Failure to Maintain Required Boundary Setbacks" |
| § 525-21(E) | R-2 rear yard: 25 ft minimum | Same |

**Penalties and who can enforce.**

- **Fines (§ 525-162):** on conviction, up to $1,250. After a 30-day cure period, up to $2,000. A repeat violation within one year draws an additional, separate fine.
- **Other remedies (§ 525-163):** the Zoning Officer "and any and all other interested persons" may seek an injunction, restraining order or mandamus. The section also recognizes the right of "any person having knowledge of the violation" to initiate prosecution.
- **Signed by:** Michael D. Minton, Director of Community Development and Zoning Office, and Officer Keith Upton, Code Enforcement Officer.
- **Copied to:** the Township Administrator, Chief of Police, Township Solicitor, Construction Office, and the NJ Cemetery Board (Executive Director Quin Archer).

**Why "we can't enforce it" doesn't hold up.**

1. **A notice is only the first step.** Under § 525-162, fines come "upon conviction... before a court." That means the Township has to file a complaint in municipal court. Ask whether it ever did. If it didn't, that is the gap.
2. **The notice already answers the grandfather claim.** It treats perimeter burials as beginning or changing a use of land. Under general NJ land-use law, nonconforming status protects a use as it existed, not an expansion of it (N.J.S.A. 40:55D-68; general knowledge, verify with counsel). Morgan raises grandfathering as a defense in court. It doesn't stop the Township from filing.
3. **Morgan's strongest counterargument.** Courts in some states treat all land dedicated to a cemetery before zoning as already "in use," even ground with no burials yet. Whether NJ courts would do that for Morgan's perimeter is the central legal question here. It depends on the dedication deed and map. If the strip was never dedicated, the argument fails, and state law also requires township consent to enlarge (N.J.S.A. 45:27-25).
4. **Burials after the notice.** Around 20 graves have been added since April 7, 2025. The notice refers to "continuous individual violations," so counsel should ask whether each burial is a separate offense.
5. **You may be able to act yourself.** § 525-163 gives "interested persons" injunction rights. The Municipal Land Use Law also lets an "interested party" sue to stop a use of land that violates a zoning ordinance (N.J.S.A. 40:55D-18; general knowledge, verify). NJ municipal courts also take citizen complaints, after a probable-cause review.
6. **The Cemetery Board ruling may not reach this issue.** The Board regulates cemetery operations, and local zoning is the Township's job. Check whether the Board's "nothing wrong" decision addressed the zoning code at all.

## Louis Cicalese: background and pattern

Cicalese has a 30-year public record of cemeteries he runs drawing complaints about how and where burials are placed. One state penalty involved him personally (1996). Since then, the record is mostly open investigations, local opposition and civil suits, not sanctions.

**Career, in his own words.** He dug his first grave at Rosemount Memorial Park in 1990. He joined the Harleigh Cemetery board in 2000. His site describes him as "advisor to the board of cemeteries" in New Jersey ([louiscicalese.com](https://www.louiscicalese.com/about-louis-cicalese/)). An attorney should ask what that advisory role is, given that the Cemetery Board later ruled on the Morgan complaint.

**Patterns that bear on Morgan Cemetery:**

1. **Crowding graves into space that isn't there (finding, 1996).** Rosemount and its two principals, Cicalese and Lawrence Nikola, paid a $60,000 fine to the Division of Consumer Affairs. The charges were that bodies were buried under sidewalks and up to nine people were put in one grave. They also admitted illegally reclaiming graves families had bought ([lehighvalleylive, summarizing the NYT](https://www.lehighvalleylive.com/news/2020/06/body-unearthed-from-warren-county-cemetery-grave-renews-call-for-state-action.html)).
2. **New rows squeezed in among existing graves (allegation, 2018–2020).** At Washington Cemetery, families said new gravesites were "intermingling with existing ones," with new rows created among existing plots. In June 2020, "old human bones" turned up with casket debris; the prosecutor called it accidental. The Cemetery Board confirmed two open complaints against the association "and its President Louis Cicalese" ([lehighvalleylive 2020](https://www.lehighvalleylive.com/news/2020/06/body-unearthed-from-warren-county-cemetery-grave-renews-call-for-state-action.html)).
3. **Work without local permits (allegation, 2018).** Washington Borough's 2018 letter said a 600-foot road was built, trees cut, and 53,000 sq ft of soil moved in without permits or borough approval (same source).
4. **Expansion pushed against neighbors (2012).** CemDevCo applied for a use variance to add 125 acres to Washington Cemetery. It withdrew three days before the hearing, after a packed meeting of residents opposed it ([lehighvalleylive 2012](https://www.lehighvalleylive.com/warren-county/express-times/2012/02/washington_cemetery_withdraws.html)).
5. **Burials on disputed land (lawsuit, 2023–2024; still disputed 2026).** In Tinton Falls, Ruffin family descendants and the Borough itself sued Cicalese and associates over Ruffin Cemetery. Plaintiffs say hundreds of remains were buried there since 2018 without authorization. After trial, the court dismissed the claims in August 2024 for lack of ownership proof, but said ownership itself was not decided ([News 12, May 2026](https://newjersey.news12.com/woman-fights-to-have-remains-of-strangers-removed-from-family-cemetery)). In 2005 board minutes, two Ruffin heirs told the Board they never authorized him to manage the cemetery; he disputed that ([Cemetery Board minutes, Sept. 2005](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/cem/Minutes/cemmin_090805.pdf)).
6. **Grave care and oversight (reports, 2013 and 2017).** In 2017 police found a body exposed atop a coffin in an unfilled grave at Rosemount; the state investigated ([NJ.com](https://www.nj.com/union/2017/07/elizabeth_cemetery_left_body_exposed_to_elements_i.html)). In 2013 Rosemount's crematory hired a man convicted in a body-parts theft scheme; he was let go after press coverage ([CBS New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/convicted-body-parts-snatcher-found-working-at-cemetery/)).

**What has not been found.** No criminal charge against Cicalese turned up. No Cemetery Board sanction after 1996 was found online. The 2013 Board action in the record was against a CemDevCo salesperson, not Cicalese, who was only copied ([2013 letter](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/Actions/20130114_47SP00137100.pdf)). Calling him "fraudulent" in a filing would go beyond this record. The safer framing is a documented pattern of complaints about burial placement and land use.

It paid that trust about $2.2 million in 2024–2025, and its cash fell from $1.82 million to $60,205. Grave sales are what repay this debt. **What the filings show:**

1. **The debt began as a "Land Share Cert" held by Cicalese personally.** The 2016 filing lists him as the lender, at $3,420,833 plus $478,917 in interest. By 2022 the creditor was "Saucha Trust," which Morgan's filings say Cicalese is trustee of. Cemetery Board minutes describe it as "a trust established for Louis Cicalese for investments" ([Board minutes, Oct. 10, 2013](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/cem/Minutes/cemmin_101013.pdf)).
2. **Most of Morgan's assets are unsold graves.** In 2016, $3.34 million of its $3.86 million in assets was "inventories for sale," meaning interment spaces. The debt is effectively paid out of grave sales.
3. **The payout to the trust sped up in 2024–2025.** Principal fell $1,320,833 and $897,833 in interest was paid in 2024, about $2.2 million in total. Interest was 69% of Morgan's 2024 expenses. Net assets fell from $1.55 million to $738,000 that year.
4. **His "related compensation" lines up with these payments.** The related pay reported for Cicalese ($235,958 in 2022; $217,925 in 2023; $141,556 in 2024) matches, or nearly matches, the interest paid to his trust or the fees paid to his company Libitina in the same year.

**The law on these certificates.** A cemetery may issue fixed-value, interest-bearing certificates to pay for land or improvements only "if the board approves." The Board must review "the dollar amount and terms of the certificates so as to assure the financial soundness of the cemetery company" ([N.J.S.A. 45:27-26](https://lis.njleg.state.nj.us/nxt/gateway.dll/statutes/1/41981/44261?f=templates$fn=document-frame.htm$3.0)). Under current law, 15% of each grave's initial sale price must go to the Maintenance and Preservation Fund (N.J.S.A. 45:27-13, as quoted in [2026 bill A602](https://www.njleg.state.nj.us/bill-search/2026/A602/bill-text?f=A1000&n=602_I1)).

## Fence-line burials: what the law allows

No state rule found sets a minimum distance between a grave and a neighbor's property line. That likely explains the Cemetery Board ruling. The stronger question is whether the fence-line graves sit on land Morgan was ever approved to use, because "grandfathered" status covers only the cemetery's existing, dedicated grounds, not new ground.

**1. State law: enlarging a cemetery needs township consent.** Under [N.J.S.A. 45:27-25](https://lis.njleg.state.nj.us/nxt/gateway.dll/statutes/1/41981/44260?f=templates$fn=document-frame.htm$3.0):

- (a) "A cemetery shall not be established or enlarged in any municipality without first obtaining the consent of the municipality by resolution."
- (e) "A cemetery company shall not dedicate additional land to cemetery purposes without board approval."

If the graves along the fence are on land added after the original dedication, Cinnaminson had to consent by resolution and the Board had to approve. If neither happened, the burials may be unlawful whatever their distance from the fence. New Jersey courts have held that towns keep the power to "prevent territorial expansion of cemeteries" and to regulate interment by ordinance ([Trinity Cemetery Ass'n v. Wall Twp.](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2318714/trinity-cemetery-assn-v-wall-tp/), decided under the earlier Cemetery Act).

**2. State regulations.** The Cemetery Board's rules, [N.J.A.C. 13:44J](<https://www.nj.gov/bpu/pdf/rules/R.2025%20d.016%20(57%20N.J.R.%20200(b)).pdf>) (readopted Dec. 24, 2024), cover licensing, trust funds, sales, disinterment, and interment-space standards. Subchapter 6 requires each cemetery to keep rules and maps of its grounds. That map is the document to demand. This review did not find a property-line setback in the rule summary; the full text of Subchapter 11 (interment spaces) should be checked.

**3. Cinnaminson zoning.** Chapter 525 has cemetery standards added by Ord. No. 2022-4 on Aug. 15, 2022 ([eCode360](https://ecode360.com/10011224)):

- Minimum lot size 25 acres.
- "A twenty-foot buffer strip shall be provided between a building and the cemetery property line."
- Accessory buildings follow single-family setbacks.

The 20-foot buffer applies to **buildings**, not graves, as written. The Township's April 2025 notice relies instead on the R-2 yard setbacks (15 ft side, 25 ft rear), the conditional-use approval requirement, and the zoning-permit rule. See the enforcement section.

**4. "Grandfathering" (pre-existing nonconforming use).** Morgan (1888) predates Cinnaminson zoning (1980) and the 2022 cemetery rules. Under the general NJ land-use rule, a lawful nonconforming use may continue but may not be expanded without a variance (Municipal Land Use Law, N.J.S.A. 40:55D-68; cited from general knowledge, not opened for this review). So the questions are:

- Was the fence-line strip inside the cemetery's dedicated, mapped grounds before 1980 and 2022?
- Was it ever used for burials before, or is this new ground? (The owner reports no graves in the strip in 2017; burials began about 1.5 years ago.)
- Does the 25-acre minimum mean Morgan is already nonconforming, so any enlargement needs a variance?

**5. How other NJ towns handle this.** Ordinances elsewhere show what Cinnaminson could adopt:

| Town | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| [North Arlington](https://ecode360.com/11268145) | "No burials or other cemetery uses within 50 feet of any property line," landscaped per a Planning Board plan |
| [Franklin Lakes](https://ecode360.com/7171095) | 250-foot buffer around a new or enlarged cemetery, planted as a visual shield for adjacent homes |
| [Ocean Township](https://ecode360.com/35341362) | Permit hearing with notice to owners within 200 feet of the boundary |

**6. The township can act.** In the Warren County matter, a Cemetery Board spokesperson said "the borough may take independent action against the cemetery for violations within its authority" ([lehighvalleylive 2020](https://www.lehighvalleylive.com/news/2020/06/body-unearthed-from-warren-county-cemetery-grave-renews-call-for-state-action.html)). The Borough of Tinton Falls joined the 2023 suit against Cicalese as plaintiff-intervenor ([Trellis](https://trellis.law/doc/187449043/correspondence-submitted-by-shapiro-lawrence-h-ansell-grimm-aaron-pc-on-behalf-louis-cicalese-michael-baratta-david-jacobs-ruffin-cemetery-corp-against-borough-tinton-falls-carrie-jones-anthony-conover-gary-herviou-michael-stanton-et-al-link)).

**7. Private claims to raise with counsel.** If a survey shows any grave, vault or marker over the line, that is trespass and encroachment, separate from cemetery law. Drainage, fence damage or equipment on your side may support nuisance or trespass claims. Whether NJDEP private-well setbacks apply is a further question for counsel if your lot has a well.

## Your fence and land: digging next to the line

Yes, there are rules, from three sources. New Jersey common law protects your land from losing support when a neighbor digs. Federal OSHA rules treat grave digging as excavation and require shoring when nearby walls or structures are put at risk. State law sets how deep each grave must be, so these are holes at least 6 ft deep, inches from your fence.

**1. How deep each grave is (state law).** The top of each coffin must be at least 4 ft below the natural surface and covered with at least 4 ft of soil, unless a sealed vault is used ([N.J.S.A. 26:6-36](https://lis.njleg.state.nj.us/nxt/gateway.dll/statutes/1/22562/24705?f=templates$fn=document-frame.htm$3.0)). Add the coffin or vault height and each grave is roughly 6 to 7 ft deep. Dug inches from your fence, that is a deep, open cut right beside your fence posts and soil.

**2. Your right to lateral support (NJ common law).**

- **Your land in its natural state has a right to support from the land next to it.** "If the land sinks or falls away in consequence of the removal of such support, the owner is entitled to damages" ([McGuire v. Grant, 25 N.J.L. 356](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/8097312/mcguire-v-grant/), quoted in [Colombo v. Peters](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7389032/colombo-v-peters/)). This does not require proving carelessness.
- **Whoever removes that support must replace it.** A landowner who removes a neighbor's natural lateral support must "supply adequate artificial support at his own expense" ([Gerhard v. Fichter](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2207193/gerhard-v-fichter/)).
- **Structures, such as your fence, are protected differently.** For a structure, the digger is liable if the excavation is careless. A landowner "intending to excavate on his own land must give the adjoining landowner notice" ([Newman v. Pasternack](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3582124/newman-v-pasternack/), citing Schultz v. Byers, 53 N.J.L. 442; [Pinto v. Foo Jang Realty](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9352871/pinto-v-foo-jang-realty-co/)). Ask whether Morgan ever gave you notice before digging.
- **The catch:** damages usually require actual harm, such as sinking soil, a leaning or shifting fence, or exposed posts. The risk of harm alone may not be enough ([Colombo](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7389032/colombo-v-peters/)). That is why documenting it now matters. These are older decisions; counsel should confirm current NJ law.

**3. Worker-safety rules (federal OSHA).** OSHA treats "grave excavation" as construction covered by its excavation standard, 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P ([OSHA interpretation, 2013](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2013-02-26)). Under [29 CFR 1926.651](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651):

- (i)(1): "Where the stability of adjoining buildings, walls, or other structures is endangered by excavation operations, support systems such as shoring, bracing, or underpinning shall be provided."
- (j)(2): excavated soil and equipment must be kept "at least 2 feet" from the edge of the hole, or held back with retaining devices.

These rules protect the cemetery's workers, not your property, and they don't give you damages. But anyone can file an OSHA complaint, and an inspection of fence-line digging without shoring is pressure the cemetery will feel. Whether your fence counts as an "other structure" is OSHA's call.

**4. Water and soil coming onto your side.** Cinnaminson has a stormwater control ordinance (Chapter 444) ([2024 ordinance](https://cinnaminsonnj.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-10-Stormwater-Control-ORD-Section-444-Cinnaminson.pdf); not reviewed in detail). If digging or new grade sends water, mud or spoil onto your lot, that is also trespass and nuisance under general NJ law.

**What to document now:**

- [ ] Photos and video of open graves next to your fence, with a tape measure showing the distance and depth.
- [ ] Any lean, gap, heave, cracked or loose posts, settling, or sinkholes on your side, dated, photographed from the same spot each time.
- [ ] Spoil piles or equipment against or on your fence, and any water or mud flowing onto your yard after rain.
- [ ] Whether anyone from the cemetery ever gave you notice before digging.
- [ ] A fence contractor's or engineer's written opinion on fence and soil stability. This turns "I'm worried" into evidence.
- [ ] Your fence's install date and cost (receipt or permit), to price any repair claim.
